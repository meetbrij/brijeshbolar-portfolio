Problem Statement

The Curated News Gap in Fintech Sales

Fintech sales teams operate in one of the most information-dense environments in financial services. Regulatory changes, funding rounds, product launches, market shifts — any of these can become a conversation opener or a competitive threat overnight.

The problem: building a curated, timely intelligence brief requires either a dedicated research function (expensive) or significant daily time from salespeople themselves (opportunity cost). Most teams default to generic news aggregators that produce noise, not signal.

This tool solves the editorial bottleneck — aggregating from targeted sources, filtering by relevance, and assembling a formatted brief personalised to each segment's coverage area, automatically.

Pain Points
  • Sales reps spending 30–45 min/day scanning news manually
  • No single source covering fintech-specific intelligence
  • Generic newsletters not tailored to specific client verticals
  • Missed opportunities when news breaks between briefings
  • No mechanism to track what news matters to which client segment
Primary Users
  • Fintech sales managers and AEs covering enterprise accounts
  • Relationship managers in banking selling fintech solutions
  • BD teams tracking competitive landscape and funding signals
Solution

How the Pipeline Works

📡
Automated Ingestion
Pulls from 50+ curated sources — fintech publications, regulatory bodies, funding databases, and company newsrooms — on a scheduled basis. No manual curation required once sources are configured.
🧠
AI Filtering & Summarisation
Each article is scored for relevance against configurable segment criteria, then summarised into a 2–3 sentence brief. Only high-signal items make it into the final digest — eliminating noise before delivery.
📬
Personalised Delivery
Each sales segment receives a formatted newsletter tailored to their coverage area — payments, lending, wealth tech, regtech — delivered on a configurable schedule (daily, weekly). Formatted for fast scanning, not deep reading.
